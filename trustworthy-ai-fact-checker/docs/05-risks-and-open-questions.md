# 5. Risks, pushback and open questions

Honest objections a buyer (or a skeptical friend) will raise, with a first answer to each.

## Pushback

- **"Two businesses in one."** The fact-checker and the governance service are different sales.
  *Answer:* lead with one. The recommended wedge is the governance pilot, with the fact-checker as
  the differentiator nobody else has; sell the engine standalone later.
- **Enterprise sales are slow.** Companies like UPS or Humana take 6–18 months and many approvals
  to buy anything. *Answer:* start with one plant, department, or a regional mid-size company.
- **Unions.** UPS (Teamsters) and Ford (UAW) sites have collective-bargaining agreements that
  govern changes to work programs and bonuses. *Answer:* bring the union in as a co-designer;
  it's likely a requirement, and could be an ally.
- **Fiduciary duty.** Boards can't hand capex decisions to an employee vote. *Answer:* bounded,
  advisory-or-funded slice, with leadership committing in advance to honor the result.
- **"Five years to vote" excludes newer staff** and may look unfair. *Answer:* consider a
  shorter bar for proposing, longer for voting, or tiered participation – decide with the pilot customer.
- **Peer accountability can turn into peer pressure.** Design a norms/conduct policy up front.
- **AI errors in front of employees destroy trust fast.** *Answer:* stakes-based human review,
  visible sources, public corrections (see `01` and `02`).
- **Data access and privacy.** Checking claims against HR or safety data requires strict
  permissions and anonymization.

## Open questions to validate (before building much)

1. Will 5–10 local HR/operations leaders say this solves a problem they'd pay for? (Interviews.)
2. What budget slice would a company realistically put to a vote?
3. Who inside the company is the buyer – CEO, CHRO, plant manager, CFO?
4. What accuracy and cost per claim does the prototype actually achieve (`03` assumptions)?
5. Can the first pilot show a measurable outcome in <12 months (savings, engagement, retention, safety)?

## Out of scope for now

The broader vision – companies collaborating instead of competing, employer-provided community
amenities, an abundance economy – is the "why" behind the movement. Keep it in the story, but the
first sale has to stand on a concrete ROI for one company.
