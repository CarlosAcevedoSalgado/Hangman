# 4. Business model: employee-voice governance, powered by verified facts

## The thesis

Employees who plan to stay at a company for years want it to succeed – their pay, bonuses,
safety and community depend on it. If you give tenured employees a real, structured say in
part of the annual plan, and make sure every proposal rests on verified facts, you get:

- better proposals (the people closest to the work see the waste and the opportunities),
- stronger ownership and peer accountability,
- investment that stays in the local community, because the voters live there.

## The offer (first version)

A 6–12 month engagement for a single site or division:

1. **Design** – eligibility (e.g. 5+ years tenure, one person one vote), which budget slice is
   votable (e.g. a fixed % of discretionary capex or improvement budget), proposal rules.
2. **Proposal pipeline** – employees submit ideas; the AI fact-checker verifies every factual claim
   in each proposal (costs, savings, safety data) against company data and public sources.
3. **Decision briefs** – each shortlisted proposal gets a one-page, fact-checked brief.
4. **Vote + results** – transparent tally; leadership commits to fund the winners.
5. **Follow-through reporting** – did the funded projects deliver what the brief claimed?
   (The fact-checker grades the outcomes too.)

The CEO / leadership still sets the overall plan and appoints department leaders; employees vote on
a defined, bounded slice. That keeps it compatible with the board's legal duties.

## Target customers

Start local, mid-to-large, community-rooted employers. Louisville-area examples: UPS (Worldport),
Humana (HQ), LG&E/KU (utility), GE Appliances (Appliance Park), Ford (Louisville Assembly and
Kentucky Truck plants). Realistically, the **first** customer is more likely a regional
manufacturer or a single plant/department of a large company than a whole Fortune 500 firm.

## Pricing and unit economics (hypotheses)

| Item | Hypothesis |
|---|---|
| Engagement price | $30K–$50K per site per year (pilot); higher for multi-site |
| Your delivery time | ~200–400 hrs in year one, dropping as the playbook and software mature |
| Software cost | Low hundreds to low thousands $/yr per customer (see `03`) |
| Effective rate | $75–$250/hr in year one, improving each repeat |
| Upsell | Standalone fact-checker for internal comms/reports as a subscription |

The leverage comes from reuse: the second customer costs far less to deliver than the first.

## Prior art to learn from (you're not starting from zero)

- **Open-book management / "The Great Game of Business"** (Jack Stack, SRC Holdings) – teaches
  all employees the financials and ties bonuses to numbers they can influence. Closest existing
  model to this idea.
- **ESOPs** (Employee Stock Ownership Plans) – employees own shares; well-studied in the US.
- **Gainsharing / Scanlon plans** – employee suggestion systems with shared savings.
- **Participatory budgeting** – citizens vote on part of a city budget; the voting mechanics transfer directly.
- **German codetermination** – workers hold seats on large companies' supervisory boards.
- **Mondragon** (Spain) – large network of worker cooperatives.

Study what made these succeed or stall; it's the fastest way to strengthen the pitch.
