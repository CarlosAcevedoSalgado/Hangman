# 9. Master plan (in stages)

Like Tesla's published "master plan": each stage funds and earns credibility for the next.
Each stage has a **gate**: a measurable result that must be true before moving on. If the gate
fails, you change the method, not the goal.

| Stage | What | Gate to move on |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Prove the engine** (0–6 mo) | Fact-checker + forecast log + scorecard as a tool. Use it on public company claims and on your own plans. | Accuracy and cost/claim measured; 5–10 leaders say they'd pay |
| **2. First pilot** (6–18 mo) | One site/department of a local mid-size employer. Employees vote on a bounded budget slice; base + success fee. | Measured result vs. a comparison group; a customer who'll be a public reference |
| **3. Repeatable playbook** (1.5–3 yrs) | 3–10 customers; software does most of the checking and scoring; hire help. | Second customer costs < half the first to deliver |
| **4. A standard** (3–5 yrs) | Publish the method as an open standard/certification ("verified employee voice"), like B Corp for decision-making. Others can adopt it; you sell the software and audits. | Companies adopting it without you in the room |
| **5. Community compact** (5+ yrs) | Certified companies in a city pool part of their gains into shared community investment, and together make a data-backed case to local government for incentives. | Measured community outcomes |

## Where I disagree with parts of the bigger vision

- **Company-provided healthcare and services replacing government.** History has a warning:
  company towns (e.g. Pullman, Illinois; Appalachian coal towns) gave employers enormous power
  over workers' lives, and US employer-tied health insurance creates "job lock": people can't
  leave a bad job without losing coverage. Anyone who isn't employed (children, retirees, people
  with disabilities, laid-off workers) falls through. A stronger version: companies invest *in
  addition to* public services, and results are measured and published.
- **Lower taxes in exchange for company spending.** Real policies exist in this direction
  (e.g. tax credits, Opportunity Zones, tax-increment financing), and their measured results are
  mixed. It's a legitimate debate, but keep it out of the first sale. Stage 5 at the earliest.
- **Peer policing of "slackers."** Peer accountability works when there is a fair, transparent
  process and it's about the work. Without that it turns into pressure, favoritism or pushing out
  people who are sick, caregiving or disagreeing. Build the conduct process before the voting.
- **"Everyone votes, not owners."** Voting without ownership is workable if the voted slice
  is clear and leadership commits in advance. But expect employees to ask why they carry
  responsibility without a share of the upside; a profit-sharing link may be needed for the idea
  to feel fair.
