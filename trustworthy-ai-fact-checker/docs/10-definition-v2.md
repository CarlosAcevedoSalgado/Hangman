# 10. Definition v2 (after session 3 feedback)

## The one sentence

A **third-party trust service**: an AI checks the claims and stated reasons behind company
decisions, from leadership and employees alike, then tracks what actually changed, so it is
safe for employees to help plan and to decide how shared gains are used.

## What changed from v1

| v1 (my framing) | v2 (Carlos's correction) |
|---|---|
| Core = employees vote on a budget slice | Core = **trust / preventing corruption**; voting only works on top of it |
| Forecasting is part of the product | Product = **claim checking + tracking what actually moved** (not forecasting) |
| Employees vote on a budget slice | Employees propose → leadership approves → people doing the work vote yes/no on the items that affect them → everyone commits ("disagree and commit") |
| Payoff unclear without ownership | Payoff = gains shared by a formula **locked before the vote**; employees choose use (machinery, bonuses, community grants) |

## What the AI can and can't check

It cannot read intentions. It checks the **stated reason**: goal → claimed mechanism
("bonuses will raise revenue — how?") → evidence in the data → tracked result afterwards.
It flags mismatches between stated reason and evidence, not the wish itself.

## Why it can work against corruption

Klitgaard (1988): Corruption = Monopoly + Discretion − Accountability.
Employees proposing and voting reduces monopoly; a locked formula reduces discretion; an outside
check on everyone's claims and results adds accountability. Employee tips are the leading way
fraud is detected (ACFE, *Report to the Nations*), so a safe flag channel belongs in the product.

## Minimum version

| Piece | v1 |
|---|---|
| Truth | **Built:** claim + stated-reason checker with sources; before/after results tracker |
| Voice | Off-the-shelf forms for suggestions and yes/no votes |
| Shared win | Signed one-page agreement with the locked sharing formula |
